# NATO Rules of Engagement — Standing ROE (Extract)

Based on publicly available ROE frameworks (NZDF, US SROE, San Remo Handbook).
This is a representative extract for testing purposes, not an actual classified document.

## Chapter I — General Provisions

### Article 1 — Scope and Application
These Rules of Engagement apply to all personnel deployed under NATO command.
All personnel must comply with these rules at all times during the mission.
National caveats may restrict but not expand the permissions granted herein.

### Article 2 — Definitions
"Hostile act" means an attack or other use of force against NATO forces or
designated persons or property.
"Hostile intent" means the threat of imminent use of force against NATO forces
or designated persons or property.
"Minimum force" means the least degree of force necessary to achieve the
authorized objective.

## Chapter II — Self-Defence

### Article 3 — Individual Self-Defence
Every service member has the inherent right to use force in self-defence.
Personnel may use force, up to and including deadly force, to defend themselves
against a hostile act or demonstrated hostile intent.
The use of force in self-defence must be proportionate to the threat.
Self-defence force shall cease once the threat has been neutralized.

### Article 4 — Unit Self-Defence
Unit commanders may authorize the use of force to defend their unit, adjacent
units, and persons under their protection against hostile acts or hostile intent.
Commanders must ensure that force used is proportionate and discriminate.
Warning shots may be fired only when authorized by the unit commander.

### Article 5 — Defence of Others
Personnel shall protect civilians and non-combatants from hostile acts.
Personnel may use force to defend designated personnel, coalition partners,
and civilians under imminent threat.
Personnel must not abandon civilians under their protection.

## Chapter III — Use of Force

### Article 6 — Escalation of Force
Personnel must follow the escalation of force procedures before employing lethal force:
(1) Shout verbal warnings in the local language and in English.
(2) Show weapon and demonstrate intent to use force.
(3) Fire warning shots into a safe area if the situation permits.
(4) Use non-lethal means if available and appropriate.
(5) Use minimum lethal force only as a last resort.
These steps may be compressed or omitted only if there is insufficient time
due to the immediacy of the threat.

### Article 7 — Prohibited Actions
Personnel are prohibited from:
(a) targeting civilians or civilian objects deliberately;
(b) using disproportionate force;
(c) employing weapons or methods that cause unnecessary suffering;
(d) conducting reprisals against prisoners of war or detained persons;
(e) destroying cultural property unless required by imperative military necessity;
(f) using human shields or forcing civilians into danger zones;
(g) engaging targets beyond the authorized area of operations without explicit authorization.

### Article 8 — Detention and Handling of Persons
Personnel may detain persons who pose a security threat within the area of operations.
Detained persons must be treated humanely at all times.
Personnel must not subject detained persons to torture, cruel treatment, or humiliation.
Detained persons shall be transferred to the appropriate authority within 96 hours.
Medical treatment shall be provided to detained persons who require it.

## Chapter IV — Specific Operations

### Article 9 — Checkpoints and Patrols
Personnel manning checkpoints may use force in accordance with the escalation
of force procedures (Article 6).
Personnel shall search persons and vehicles entering the secured area.
Personnel must not use collective punishment against civilian populations
at checkpoints.

### Article 10 — Rules for Air Operations
Pilots must positively identify targets before engagement.
Collateral damage must be assessed before every strike.
Strikes are prohibited within 500 meters of hospitals, schools, and religious sites
unless authorized by the Joint Force Commander.
Close air support must be coordinated with ground forces before engagement.

### Article 11 — Maritime Operations
Vessels may be boarded and inspected within the designated maritime area.
Warning shots across the bow may be fired to compel compliance.
The use of force against non-compliant vessels must be proportionate.
Personnel shall not fire on vessels that have surrendered or are clearly disabled.

## Chapter V — Reporting and Accountability

### Article 12 — Reporting Obligations
Every use of force must be reported within 24 hours to the chain of command.
All incidents involving civilian casualties must be reported immediately.
Commanders must investigate all reports of ROE violations.
Personnel must not destroy evidence of ROE violations.

### Article 13 — Commander's Responsibilities
Commanders are responsible for ensuring their subordinates understand these rules.
Commanders must conduct ROE briefings before every operation.
Commanders who knowingly order ROE violations shall be held accountable.
Failure to prevent subordinates from committing ROE violations constitutes
a breach of command responsibility.
