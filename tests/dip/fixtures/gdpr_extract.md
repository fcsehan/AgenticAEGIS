# GDPR — General Data Protection Regulation: test extract

English adaptation for pipeline tests, not an authoritative legal edition.

## Chapter II — Principles

### Article 5 — Principles for processing personal data

(1) Personal data must:
a) be processed lawfully, fairly and transparently in relation to the data subject (lawfulness, fairness and transparency);
b) be collected for specified, explicit and legitimate purposes and must not be further processed incompatibly with those purposes (purpose limitation);
c) be adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary for processing purposes (data minimization);
d) be accurate and, where necessary, current; reasonable measures must ensure that inaccurate data are erased or corrected without undue delay (accuracy);
e) be stored in a form allowing identification for no longer than the processing purposes require (storage limitation);
f) be processed with appropriate security (integrity and confidentiality).

(2) The controller is responsible for compliance with Paragraph 1 and must demonstrate compliance (accountability).

### Article 6 — Lawfulness of processing

(1) Processing is lawful only if at least one of these conditions holds:
a) The data subject has consented to processing for one or more specific purposes;
b) processing is necessary for a contract with the data subject or for steps before entering a contract;
c) processing is necessary for a legal obligation binding the controller;
d) processing is necessary to protect vital interests of the data subject or another person;
e) processing is necessary for a task in the public interest;
f) processing is necessary for legitimate interests pursued by the controller or a third party.

## Chapter III — Rights of the data subject

### Article 13 — Information when collecting personal data from the data subject

(1) When collecting personal data, the controller shall give the data subject:
a) the controller's name and contact details;
b) contact details for the data protection officer;
c) processing purposes and the legal basis for processing;
d) legitimate interests pursued by the controller or a third party;
e) recipients or categories of recipients;
f) any intention to transfer personal data to a third country.

### Article 17 — Right to erasure (right to be forgotten)

(1) The data subject has the right to request erasure without undue delay. The controller must erase the data without undue delay if one of these grounds applies:
a) The data are no longer needed for the purposes for which they were collected or processed.
b) The data subject withdraws consent under Article 6 Paragraph 1 point a and no other legal basis supports processing.
c) The data subject objects to processing under Article 21 Paragraph 1.

(2) If the controller made the data public and must erase them under Paragraph 1, the controller shall take reasonable measures, considering available technology and costs, to inform other controllers that the data subject requested erasure of links to the data.

(3) Paragraphs 1 and 2 do not apply where processing is necessary:
a) for freedom of expression and information;
b) for a legal obligation under Union or member-state law;
c) for public-interest reasons in public health;
d) for public-interest archiving;
e) to establish, exercise or defend legal claims.

## Chapter IV — Controller and processor

### Article 32 — Security of processing

(1) The controller and processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures, considering available technology, costs, processing context and risks to people's rights and freedoms. Measures include:
a) pseudonymization and encryption;
b) ongoing confidentiality, integrity, availability and resilience of processing systems and services;
c) timely restoration of data availability and access after a physical or technical incident;
d) regular testing and evaluation of the effectiveness of security measures.

(2) Security assessment must consider processing risks, including destruction, loss, alteration or unauthorized disclosure of personal data.
